Lesson 1 Summary

  • Quadratic Equations, and by extension Quadratic Functions, are polynomial expressions with a second power on one of the coefficients.
  • In every Quadratic Equation, there are two roots or values of x that satisfy the equation.
  • There are three ways to solve a Quadratic Equation, each with their own uses.
  • In Quadratic Functions, they are graphed into a U-shaped curve called a Parabola.
  • The roots of the function are known as the x-intercepts while a point in the curve where it touches the y-axis is called a y-intercept.
  • The General Form of a Function can help find its x-intercepts, while the Standard Form can determine how the graph is transformed.
